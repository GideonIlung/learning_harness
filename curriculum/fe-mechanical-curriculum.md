## 1. Dynamics, Kinematics & Vibrations

**Exam weight:** 10–15 questions  
**Planned time:** 14 hours

### Official scope
- Kinematics of particles
- Kinetic friction
- Newton’s second law for particles
- Work-energy of particles
- Impulse-momentum of particles
- Kinematics of rigid bodies
- Kinematics of mechanisms
- Newton’s second law for rigid bodies
- Work-energy of rigid bodies
- Impulse-momentum of rigid bodies
- Free and forced vibrations

### Learning objectives
By the end, be able to:
- Choose between kinematics, Newton's second law, work-energy, and impulse-momentum based on the problem structure.
- Draw correct motion diagrams and free-body diagrams for particles and rigid bodies.
- Relate translation and rotation using angular kinematics and rigid-body constraints.
- Analyze simple vibration systems using natural frequency, forcing, damping, and resonance ideas.

### Key checkpoints
- Can you identify whether time, displacement, velocity, acceleration, energy, or impulse is the most useful bridge?
- Can you explain when energy methods are easier than force-acceleration methods?
- Can you distinguish particle dynamics from rigid-body dynamics?

## 2. Statics

**Exam weight:** 9–14 questions  
**Planned time:** 10 hours

### Official scope
- Resultants of force systems
- Concurrent force systems
- Equilibrium of rigid bodies
- Frames and trusses
- Centroids and moments of inertia
- Static friction

### Learning objectives
- Draw complete free-body diagrams with correct forces, moments, supports, and reactions.
- Reduce force systems to resultants and moments.
- Apply equilibrium equations to particles, rigid bodies, trusses, and frames.
- Compute centroids and area moments of inertia using composite shapes and parallel-axis theorem.
- Analyze impending motion using static friction limits.

### Key checkpoints
- Can you isolate the correct body before writing equilibrium equations?
- Can you tell the difference between a force, moment, couple, and resultant?
- Can you recognize two-force and zero-force members in trusses?

## 3. Mechanics of Materials

**Exam weight:** 9–14 questions  
**Planned time:** 12 hours

### Official scope
- Shear and moment diagrams
- Stress transformations and Mohr's circle
- Stress and strain caused by axial loads
- Stress and strain caused by bending loads
- Stress and strain caused by torsional loads
- Stress and strain caused by shear
- Stress and strain caused by temperature changes
- Combined loading
- Deformations
- Column buckling
- Statically indeterminate systems

### Learning objectives
- Convert loads into internal forces and moments using sections.
- Build and interpret shear-force and bending-moment diagrams.
- Compute normal stress, shear stress, strain, deformation, and angle of twist.
- Use transformation equations/Mohr’s circle for principal stresses and maximum shear.
- Analyze combined loading and compare against allowable limits.
- Recognize column buckling and statically indeterminate cases.

### Key checkpoints
- Can you move from external loading → internal resultants → stress → strain/deformation?
- Can you explain why bending stress varies linearly through a beam depth?
- Can you identify the critical section and critical stress state?

## 4. Mechanical Design & Analysis

**Exam weight:** 10–15 questions  
**Planned time:** 13 hours

### Official scope
- Stress analysis of machine elements
- Failure theories and analysis
- Deformation and stiffness
- Springs
- Pressure vessels and piping
- Bearings
- Power screws
- Power transmission
- Joining methods: welding, adhesives, mechanical fasteners
- Manufacturability: limits, fits
- Quality and reliability
- Components: hydraulic, pneumatic, electromechanical
- Engineering drawing interpretations and GD&T

### Learning objectives
- Apply stress and failure concepts to machine components.
- Use factor of safety, allowable stress, and failure theories appropriately.
- Analyze springs, pressure vessels, bearings, fasteners, welds, shafts, gears, belts, and screws at FE depth.
- Interpret fits, tolerances, engineering drawings, and GD&T basics.
- Recognize reliability/quality terminology likely to appear in exam problems.

### Key checkpoints
- Can you choose the right failure criterion for ductile vs brittle material?
- Can you distinguish strength-limited design from stiffness-limited design?
- Can you identify when a problem is really a mechanics-of-materials problem in machine-design clothing?

## 5. Electricity & Magnetism

**Exam weight:** 5–8 questions  
**Planned time:** 6 hours

### Official scope
- Electrical fundamentals: charge, current, voltage, resistance, power, energy, magnetic flux
- DC circuit analysis: Kirchhoff's laws, Ohm's law, series, parallel
- AC circuit analysis: resistors, capacitors, inductors
- Motors and generators

### Learning objectives
- Use Ohm’s law, power relations, and energy relations fluently.
- Analyze simple DC circuits with series/parallel reduction, KCL, and KVL.
- Recognize AC impedance behavior of R, L, and C elements.
- Understand basic motor/generator power and efficiency relationships.

### Key checkpoints
- Can you distinguish voltage, current, resistance, power, and energy conceptually and by units?
- Can you decide when to use KCL vs KVL?
- Can you compute real power and efficiency in simple electrical machines?

## 6. Material Properties & Processing

**Exam weight:** 7–11 questions  
**Planned time:** 8 hours

### Official scope
- Properties: chemical, electrical, mechanical, physical, thermal
- Stress-strain diagrams
- Ferrous metals
- Nonferrous metals
- Engineered materials: composites, polymers
- Manufacturing processes
- Phase diagrams, phase transformation, and heat treating
- Materials selection
- Corrosion mechanisms and control
- Failure mechanisms: thermal failure, fatigue, fracture, creep

### Learning objectives
- Interpret stress-strain diagrams and extract mechanical properties.
- Compare metals, polymers, composites, and engineered materials.
- Read basic phase diagrams and connect heat treatment to properties.
- Match manufacturing processes to material/process constraints.
- Recognize corrosion and failure modes from descriptions.

### Key checkpoints
- Can you distinguish strength, stiffness, toughness, ductility, hardness, and resilience?
- Can you explain what heat treatment changes microscopically and mechanically?
- Can you match likely failure mode to loading/time/temperature environment?

## 7. Thermodynamics

**Exam weight:** 10–15 questions  
**Planned time:** 12 hours

### Official scope
- Properties of ideal gases and pure substances
- Energy transfers
- Laws of thermodynamics
- Processes
- Performance of components
- Power cycles
- Refrigeration and heat pump cycles
- Nonreacting mixtures of gases
- Psychrometrics
- HVAC processes
- Combustion and combustion products

### Learning objectives
- Identify the system/control volume, process, assumptions, and property model.
- Use property tables/charts and ideal-gas relations correctly.
- Apply first-law energy balances to closed systems and steady-flow devices.
- Use second-law ideas, entropy, efficiency, COP, and isentropic processes.
- Analyze basic power, refrigeration, heat pump, psychrometric, HVAC, and combustion problems.

### Key checkpoints
- Can you decide whether the problem is closed-system or control-volume?
- Can you choose the right property source: ideal gas, steam table, refrigerant table, or psychrometric chart?
- Can you explain what assumptions make a process isentropic?

## 8. Fluid Mechanics

**Exam weight:** 10–15 questions  
**Planned time:** 8 hours

### Official scope
- Fluid properties
- Fluid statics
- Energy, impulse, and momentum
- Internal flow
- External flow
- Compressible flow: Mach number, isentropic flow relationships, normal shock
- Power and efficiency
- Performance curves
- Scaling laws for fans, pumps, and compressors

### Learning objectives
- Use fluid properties and units consistently.
- Solve hydrostatics problems involving pressure variation and forces on surfaces.
- Apply continuity, Bernoulli/energy equation, and momentum equations.
- Analyze pipe flow losses and internal-flow regimes.
- Use pump/fan/compressor power, efficiency, curves, and scaling laws.
- Recognize basic compressible-flow quantities and relationships.

### Key checkpoints
- Can you identify what is conserved: mass, energy, or momentum?
- Can you explain when Bernoulli is valid and what terms modify it?
- Can you distinguish pressure head, velocity head, elevation head, and head loss?

## 9. Heat Transfer

**Planned time:** 8 hours

### Official scope
- Conduction
- Convection
- Radiation
- Transient processes
- Heat exchangers

### Learning objectives
- Model heat flow using thermal resistance networks.
- Apply Fourier’s law, Newton’s law of cooling, and radiation exchange relations.
- Distinguish steady vs transient heat transfer.
- Use lumped-capacitance ideas when justified.
- Analyze heat exchangers using LMTD/effectiveness concepts at FE depth.

### Key checkpoints

- Can you build the equivalent thermal-resistance diagram?

## 10. Measurements, Instrumentation & Controls

**Exam weight:** 5–8 questions  
**Planned time:** 5 hours

### Official scope
- Sensors and transducers
- Control systems: feedback, block diagrams
- Dynamic system response
- Measurement uncertainty: error propagation, accuracy, precision, significant figures

### Learning objectives
- Interpret sensor/transducer behavior and measurement terminology.
- Use accuracy, precision, resolution, and uncertainty correctly.
- Propagate uncertainty through simple calculations.
- Read control block diagrams and identify open-loop/closed-loop behavior.
- Connect first-/second-order response parameters to time-domain behavior.

### Key checkpoints
- Can you distinguish accuracy from precision?
- Can you combine independent uncertainties correctly?
- Can you interpret feedback as error correction rather than merely signal routing?

## 11. Engineering Economics

**Exam weight:** 4–6 questions  
**Planned time:** 4 hours

### Official scope
- Time value of money: equivalence, present worth, equivalent annual worth, future worth, rate of return, annuities
- Cost types and breakdowns: fixed, variable, incremental, average, sunk
- Economic analyses: cost-benefit, breakeven, minimum cost, overhead, life cycle

### Learning objectives
- Draw cash-flow diagrams and choose the correct equivalence relation.
- Compute present worth, future worth, annual worth, and rate-based quantities.
- Distinguish fixed, variable, incremental, average, and sunk costs.
- Perform breakeven and minimum-cost comparisons.

### Key checkpoints
- Can you place each cash flow at the correct time?
- Can you explain why sunk costs should not affect a future decision?
- Can you choose PW vs AW vs FW based on what is being compared?

- Measures of central tendencies and dispersions: mean, mode, standard deviation, confidence intervals

# Suggested Session Pattern for Each Topic

For each topic, use this learning loop:

1. **Diagnostic:** 5–8 quick questions to identify fragile prerequisites.
2. **Opening problem:** one FE-style problem that exposes why the topic’s main method is needed.
3. **Core concepts:** concise explanation with equations, diagrams, and FE Handbook references.
4. **Worked example:** one fully worked problem, emphasizing method choice.
5. **Guided practice:** learner completes missing steps.
6. **Independent practice:** mixed FE-style problems.
7. **Transfer checkpoint:** a problem that looks different but uses the same idea.
8. **Review note:** record formulas, traps, and missed concepts.

# Priority Notes

Because the spreadsheet starts with high-weight mechanical fundamentals, early study should emphasize:

- free-body diagrams
- sign conventions
- conservation principles
- units and dimensions
- method selection
- FE Handbook navigation

These skills transfer strongly across dynamics, statics, mechanics of materials, machine design, fluids, thermo, and heat transfer.
