# French Lesson Notes — Basics

## Pronunciation preference
Use simple English-style pronunciation approximations for new French words and phrases.

## Articles and noun gender
French nouns have gender. Learn nouns with their article.

| Meaning | French | Pronunciation |
|---|---|---|
| the book | **le livre** | “luh LEE-vruh” |
| the house | **la maison** | “lah meh-ZON” |
| the books/houses | **les livres / les maisons** | “lay LEE-vruh / lay meh-ZON” |
| a book | **un livre** | “uhn LEE-vruh” |
| a house | **une maison** | “oon meh-ZON” |
| some books/houses | **des livres / des maisons** | “day LEE-vruh / day meh-ZON” |

## Adjective agreement with *petit*
Adjectives often match the noun in gender and number.

| Form | Example | Pronunciation |
|---|---|---|
| masculine singular | **un petit livre** | “uhn puh-TEE LEE-vruh” |
| feminine singular | **une petite maison** | “oon puh-TEET meh-ZON” |
| masculine plural | **des petits livres** | “day puh-TEE LEE-vruh” |
| feminine plural | **des petites maisons** | “day puh-TEET meh-ZON” |

## Present tense: *parler* = to speak
| French | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **je parle** | “zhuh parl” | I speak |
| **nous parlons** | “noo par-LON” | we speak |
| **vous parlez** | “voo par-LAY” | you speak |

## Useful verbs
| French | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **je suis** | “zhuh swee” | I am |
| **j’ai** | “zhay” | I have |
| **est** | “eh” | is |
| **sont** | “son” | are |

Note: **sont** = are; **son** = his/her/its. They sound alike.

## Negation
French wraps the verb with **ne ... pas**.

- **Je ne parle pas français.** — “zhuh nuh parl pah frahn-SEH” = I don’t speak French.
- **Je n’ai pas de livre.** — “zhuh nay pah duh LEE-vruh” = I don’t have a book.

After negation, **un/une/des** usually become **de**:

- **J’ai une petite maison.** → **Je n’ai pas de petite maison.**

## Questions with *où*
- **où** — “oo” = where
- **Où est la gare ?** — “oo eh lah gar” = Where is the station?

Accent note: learner may omit accents while typing; interpret `ou` as **où** when context means “where”.

## This/that/these/those: demonstratives
| Use | French | Example | Pronunciation |
|---|---|---|---|
| masculine singular | **ce** | **ce livre** | “suh LEE-vruh” |
| masculine before vowel | **cet** | **cet étudiant** | “set ay-too-DYAHN” |
| feminine singular | **cette** | **cette maison** | “set meh-ZON” |
| plural | **ces** | **ces livres** | “say LEE-vruh” |

French **ce/cette/ces** can mean this/that/these/those depending on context.

## Here / there
| French | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **ici** | “ee-SEE” | here |
| **là** | “lah” | there |

## Sentences practiced
- **Ce petit livre est ici.** — “suh puh-TEE LEE-vruh eh ee-SEE” = This small book is here.
- **Cette petite maison est ici.** — “set puh-TEET meh-ZON eh ee-SEE” = This small house is here.
- **Ces petits livres sont ici.** — “say puh-TEE LEE-vruh son ee-SEE” = These small books are here.
- **Ces petites maisons sont ici.** — “say puh-TEET meh-ZON son ee-SEE” = These small houses are here.

## Common mistakes to review
- Missing plural **-s** after **ces**: **ces petits livres**, **ces petites maisons**.
- Writing **son** instead of **sont** for “are”.
- Using **un/une/des** after negation instead of **de**.
- Missing accents is acceptable for now, but correct forms include **où** and **français**.
